\spechead{Portfolio Accounting \& Performance Reporting}{portfolio-accounting}

The system of record for holdings, transactions, and returns, plus the
reporting surface advisors and clients consume. The category's anchor
component --- every incumbent leads with it.

\specsection{Advertised capabilities (feature level)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Daily portfolio accounting: positions, transactions, cost basis, tax lots.
  \item Performance calculation (time- and money-weighted returns) at account,
        sleeve, and household levels.
  \item Multi-currency and multi-asset-class support, including complex ownership
        structures.
  \item Drag-and-drop custom report builder; pre-built templates; white-labeled,
        firm-branded output.
  \item Real-time / on-demand recalculation; delivery as PDF, client portal, and mobile.
\end{itemize}

\begin{ratiobox}
\textbf{Ratio mapping.} This is the heart of Ratio's \emph{trusted core}.
Holdings and transactions are integer-vector entries over the conserved-dimension
basis (currencies, positions, tax lots, P\&L); the ledger is the proven monoidal
fold. Performance and report aggregations are read-only projections over the
append-only log via the gRPC \texttt{Ledger} API. Where incumbents \emph{assert}
the accounting is right, Ratio's is \emph{machine-checked} (\,see the whitepaper,
\emph{A Formally Grounded Accounting Kernel}\,) and bit-exactly replayable.
\end{ratiobox}

\specsection{Conservation \& trust placement}
\textbf{Trusted core (proven).} The accounting kernel; report rendering and
return formulas sit just above it as deterministic projections. Report
\emph{templates} are control-plane artifacts (versioned, content-addressed).
