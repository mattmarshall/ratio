The problem

The incumbent stack runs on trust, not proof

For two decades, advisors, family offices, and asset managers have standardized on a handful of portfolio-accounting and performance-reporting platforms. These systems are capable and entrenched. They are also, structurally, black boxes: closed engines whose correctness you cannot inspect, whose history you cannot reproduce independently, and whose roadmap and pricing you do not control.

That arrangement was acceptable when there was no alternative. It is increasingly expensive in four concrete ways.

Correctness is asserted, not demonstrated

The arithmetic behind a performance figure or a tax lot is proprietary. When custodian, platform, and spreadsheet disagree, the resolution is manual reconciliation, not a proof. Operations teams exist largely to paper over a trust gap.

History is not reproducible

Restating a prior period — or answering “what did we report on this date, and under which rules?” — means reconstructing state from logs and memory. Late-arriving prices and silent rule changes can alter the past without a trace.

You are locked in, and billed on your success

Data lives in the vendor's format and integrations route through the vendor's gates. Pricing is typically a function of assets under management, so the bill rises with your book even when your usage does not.

AI is bolted on, not built in safely

The incumbents are racing to add LLM features atop engines never designed to contain them — exactly the architecture in which a confident, wrong model can quietly move a number.

The core problem isn't features — it's foundations. You can buy more reports, more dashboards, more integrations. You cannot buy, from any incumbent, a ledger you are able to verify.

The thesis

Accounting's mechanical core is a conservation law

Conservation laws are exactly the kind of thing one proves once and relies on forever. A transaction is an integer vector over a basis of conserved dimensions — currencies, positions, tax lots, P&L. It is valid if and only if it nets to zero on every dimension. The ledger is a deterministic fold of the append-only log, and conservation is closed under that fold.

So the ledger conserves value regardless of business semantics. Posting rules, account mappings, and normal-balance conventions — the parts that change constantly — live outside the proven core, where changing them cannot break the arithmetic.

The name comes from the same idea. In Roman finance ratio meant not just proportion but account and reckoning; citizens were asked to present their books in symmetrical tablets and achieve parem rationem — the demand that credits and debits balance exactly. That symmetry is the whole invariant, and now it is a theorem.

What follows from it

Five differences a reporting layer cannot retrofit

Ratio does not add a layer on top of the same kind of engine. It replaces the engine.

Provable correctness

The kernel's invariant is machine-checked in Lean 4, and the Rust engine is generated from the proven source. Correctness is established for all inputs by a proof assistant, not sampled by a test suite.

Deterministic audit

An append-only log of integer-exact entries, folded deterministically. No floating-point drift, no order-dependence, no hidden mutable state. Any past state replays bit-for-bit under the rules in force then.

AI-native, safely

Language models turn intent into precise specifications and draft client-ready explanations. They sit above a hard trust boundary and are checked against the kernel before anything reaches a ledger.

Readable, and checkable

The kernel is readable Rust, its proofs ship with it, and the contract is documented — so your reviewers, your auditors and your regulator can check the arithmetic rather than take it on trust. Your history exports in open formats. The engine is source-available, not permissively licensed: you can audit it, and you cannot resell it.

Fast because it is simple

A non-expressive kernel over fixed-width integer records is branch-minimal: its cost is set by memory bandwidth, not by how many business cases the engine must dispatch on.

The contrast

A glass box, not a black box

What “formally verified” actually buys

Proof, not promises

For a wealth platform this is not an academic flourish. It is the most practical guarantee a buyer can get.

The ledger cannot silently go out of balance

Assemble a ledger from valid transactions and the ledger is balanced — proved for every possible sequence of inputs. Not “we tested the common cases”: the failure mode every accounting system fears is ruled out mathematically.

The engine you run is the engine that was proved

Production code is generated from the verified source, with a build gate that fails if the two ever diverge. There is no gap between the model reasoned about and the software that shipped — the classic place where verified systems quietly rot.

Exactness, not tolerance

Money is integer minor units, never floating point. Sums are associative and exact. There is no rounding epsilon to tune and no end-of-day pass to absorb drift. The number is the number.

Reproducibility you can hand to an auditor

Inputs are immutable and rules are pinned by content hash, so any prior state replays bit-for-bit. “Show me exactly how this figure was computed, under the policy in force that quarter” is a deterministic re-run, not an investigation.

The buyer's translation. Fewer reconciliation FTEs. Faster, cleaner audits. Restatements that are provably correct. A platform whose central numbers you can defend to a regulator, a client, or a court — because they are backed by a proof, not a brand.

The economics of certainty

A bill that stops tracking your book

Incumbent platforms typically price as a function of assets under management. That couples your technology bill to your investment success — a tax on growth that has nothing to do with the cost of serving you. Layered on top are implementation fees, per-seat add-ons, and the soft cost of the operations headcount that exists to reconcile a system you cannot verify.

Ratio inverts the model. A core priced on usage means platform cost flattens as the book grows, and exact, reproducible accounting shrinks the reconciliation burden rather than feeding it.

Cost decoupled from AUM

Doubling your book does not double your platform bill.

Lower operational drag

Exactness and bit-exact replay remove whole categories of reconciliation and restatement work.

No lock-in premium

Open export formats and the option to run it in your own environment mean your exit cost — and therefore your negotiating leverage — is fundamentally different.

AI you can actually trust

The model proposes. The proven kernel disposes.

Every platform in the category is now shipping AI. The question that matters is not whether a vendor has a language model — it is where the model sits relative to your numbers. Bolt a generative model onto a black-box ledger and you have added a confident, occasionally-wrong author with write access to financial truth.

Above the boundary — what a model may do

Translate an advisor's plain-language intent into a precise, typed specification. Draft client-ready explanations of a position, a fee, or a performance result. Propose posting rules, mappings, and reconciliations for human review.

Below it — what no model can do

Write an entry that violates conservation. Every proposal is compiled and checked against the kernel's invariant before it can affect a ledger; anything that fails to conserve value is rejected at the boundary. The worst case for a hallucination is a discarded suggestion — never an unbalanced book.

Net: the productivity of an AI copilot for the parts that are genuinely language problems — intent, explanation, drafting — with a mathematical guarantee that the model can never be the reason a number is wrong.

Scoped deliberately: the kernel proves conservation. Authorization and access control are a separate concern and are not claimed as kernel theorems.

Where Ratio is today

What is proved, and what is still specified

Ratio is an early-stage project with an unusually finished foundation and a deliberately unfinished surface. The distinction matters more here than it would elsewhere — a platform that asks you to verify rather than trust should start by being precise about itself.

Proved and running

  • The conservation kernel, machine-checked in Lean 4 — the ledger fold preserves conservation, and classical double-entry is recovered as the one-dimensional case.
  • Proof-to-artifact emission: the Rust is generated from the Lean source, gated by a build test that fails if proof and artifact diverge.
  • Integer-exact money with proven associativity, identity, and inverse — no floating point anywhere in the core.
  • An AIP-linted gRPC contract and a server that refuses any transaction failing to conserve.

Designed and specified

  • The content-addressed control plane and the typed fact plane — architected in the whitepaper, not yet implemented.
  • Persistence, multi-currency, tax lots, and performance reporting — specified in the repository, on the roadmap.
  • Terminal UI, client portal, Python extension surface, and custodian integrations.
  • Sixteen competitive component specifications mapping the incumbent feature surface onto this architecture.

The claims elsewhere on this page describe the architecture and the proofs that exist today; the product surface around them is being built against the roadmap above. The kernel, the emission pipeline, and the proofs are real and readable now — start there.
