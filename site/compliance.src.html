Compliance and audit

A click and a query. Not an audit.

Most of what an examination costs is not judgment — it is assembly. Finding out what the system did, under which rules, on which day, and persuading someone that the reconstruction is faithful.

Ratio does not make audits easier by producing better reports. It makes them easier because the evidence is a by-product of how the system runs, not something anyone has to go and build afterwards.

Why audit season costs what it does

The answers exist. They are just not stored anywhere.

An examiner's questions are almost always about the past: what was reported, under what policy, on whose authority, and does it still come out the same. A conventional platform can tell you what it believes today. Everything else is reconstructed from logs, tickets, email and the memory of whoever was there.

Reconstruction is not evidence

A spreadsheet assembled after the fact to explain a figure is an argument about the past, not a record of it. It is only as good as the person who built it, and it has to be rebuilt for the next question.

Rules changed and nothing recorded when

A fee schedule was amended, a classification convention shifted, a threshold moved. If configuration lives in screens that authorized people edit, then the question "what rules were in force in March?" has no authoritative answer.

Nobody can re-run the past

Re-running a prior period against today's system gives today's answers. Proving what was reported then means trusting the archive of the report rather than the system that produced it.

The burden lands on your best people

The staff who can answer examination questions are the ones who understand the fund — which is to say, the ones you least want spending six weeks assembling binders.

What changes

Five properties, and every one of them is structural

None of these are compliance features bolted on. They fall out of decisions made for other reasons — which is why they cannot be switched off, forgotten, or configured wrong.

Every figure names the rules that made it

Each posting, position, NAV and reconciliation difference carries the hash of the configuration version it was produced under, and for a difference that includes the threshold it was measured against — so "was this material?" resolves the same way "which rules generated this number?" does. A difference is measured against the configuration its reconciliation ran under, not against whatever is in force today.

Every rule change is a signed, checked commit

Who proposed it, who approved it, which checks passed, when it takes effect, and what it did to the prior period when replayed. That record is created because it is how a change is made — not because someone remembered to log it.

The past re-runs exactly

Inputs are immutable and the rules are pinned, so replaying 30 June under the June configuration produces the June numbers — to the cent, today or in seven years. The archived report and the system agree because the system can still produce it.

Nothing can be quietly amended

The record is append-only. A correction is a new fact with its own provenance and its own timestamp, sitting alongside what it corrects. There is no edit path to audit, because there is no edit.

An accepted difference is a record, not a deletion

When somebody decides a reconciliation difference is acceptable, the reason is recorded against it with their name, the date, the figure it was about and the threshold it was measured against. The item keeps its address and its place in the queue, marked explained. If a later reconciliation reports a different figure the note steps aside and says what moved, so an explanation cannot outlive the number it was given about.

Show, don't tell

Six questions an examiner actually asks

Not paraphrases — these are the shapes that turn into six-week exercises. Each one resolves against the record rather than against somebody's reconstruction of it.

“Show me the NAV as at 30 June, and the rules in force when it was struck.”

Question three

“Demonstrate the ledger balanced at every point in the period.”

  • Not a sample, and not a reconciliation report — the balancing condition is checked on every transaction before it is admitted, so an unbalanced state is not a thing the system can be in.
  • The check itself is the subject of a machine-checked proof, which your reviewers can inspect and verify independently. See the technical page.
  • Replaying the epoch re-establishes it over the whole period rather than at period end, and produces the same result every time it is run.

The difference decomposes into a rule change and a data revision because both are first-class records with timestamps. Nobody had to work out which it was.

Question five

“What could this person have changed, and did they?”

  • Configuration changes are the only way policy moves, and each carries its proposer and approver by name.
  • Nobody can change a number directly. A figure is a function of immutable facts and a pinned configuration; there is no path that edits a posting in place.
  • Approval and authorship are separable, so four-eyes is a property of the record rather than a policy people are asked to follow.
  • Access control and entitlement review are a genuine part of this question, and they sit outside the proven core — see the limits below.

Nothing was cleared, so nothing has to be reconstructed. The item kept its address, its figures and its place in the queue, and the note against it stopped applying the moment the number moved rather than when somebody noticed.

The common thread: none of these answers is produced for the examiner. They are how the system already works, exposed. That is what makes the response a query rather than a project — and it is also why the answer does not depend on who is available that week.

Where it lands

The same property, in five different rooms

The annual audit

Sampling still happens, but the sample resolves against a record that re-runs rather than a reconstruction that has to be defended. Fewer rounds, and fewer of them landing on your controller.

Evidence by construction

Regulatory examination

“As at” questions and “under which policy” questions are the two expensive shapes, and both are direct queries.

Evidence by construction

Investor due diligence

An operational due-diligence questionnaire asks how you know your numbers are right. Being able to show the mechanism rather than describe the controls is a different conversation.

Evidence by construction

Restatement

When something has to be restated, the prior position is reproducible and the cause decomposes into rule changes and data revisions — so the restatement is explainable rather than merely correct.

Deterministic replay

Internal control testing

Segregation between authorship and approval, and the impossibility of an in-place edit, are structural facts a tester can verify once rather than behaviors they have to sample.

Control by construction

Change management

Every policy change carries its proposal, review, checks, approver and effective epoch. The change log is not a parallel artifact that can drift from what actually happened.

Control plane

Limits, stated plainly

What this page is not claiming

A compliance page that overstates is precisely the document that gets read back to you. So, explicitly:

What Ratio provides

  • Evidence that is generated by the system's ordinary operation and cannot be produced retrospectively.
  • Reproducibility of any prior state under the rules then in force.
  • An immutable, attributable record of every policy change.
  • A correctness property for balancing that is proved rather than tested.

What it does not

  • It is not a compliance program, and it does not discharge any obligation. It produces evidence; your people and processes satisfy the requirement.
  • It carries no certification or regulatory approval, and nothing on this site should be read as an assurance opinion.
  • It does not perform investor onboarding, sanctions or anti-money-laundering screening, trade surveillance, or regulatory filing.
  • The proved property is balancing. It is not a proof that a rule is the right rule — a correctly-balanced posting can still implement a policy incorrectly, which is what review and replay are for.
  • Access control and entitlement sit outside the proven core and carry the assurance of ordinary well-built software, not of a theorem.

And the largest limit of all: most of what this page describes is designed and specified rather than built. The balancing proof and the engine that enforces it exist today, and so does the reconciliation record in question six — differences measured against a declared threshold, explanations recorded by name, and a fund that refuses its own NAV while one is unexplained. The control plane, replay tooling and most of the interfaces shown here do not. See the roadmap for exactly what is where.
