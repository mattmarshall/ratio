Roadmap

The foundation is finished. The surface is not.

Ratio inverts the usual order. Most platforms ship breadth first and spend the next decade trying to make the core trustworthy. This one started with the part that cannot be retrofitted, which means the remaining work is the part everybody already knows how to do.

Below is every capability the incumbents advertise, where it sits relative to the proven core, and the order it gets built in. Nothing here is dated — see the note at the foot of the page.

Where things stand

Honest arithmetic on how far along this is

Built and working

  • The conservation kernel, with its central invariant machine-checked in Lean 4.
  • Generation of the running engine from the proved source, gated so the two cannot drift apart.
  • Exact integer money, with associativity, identity and inverse proved rather than assumed.
  • A linted service contract, and a server that refuses any transaction which fails to balance.
  • The append-only journal — persistence without a database. The log is the store; a trial balance is a fold of it.
  • Multi-currency, with conservation per currency and FX translation on every read.
  • Tax lots and the cost-basis methods a book elects, and corporate actions in factor form — nothing in the journal is rewritten.
  • Reconciliation against a counterparty file, with each difference measured against the fund's declared threshold.
  • That threshold as a term of the configuration rather than a constant in the software — declared or taken by custom, and the reconciliation reports which.
  • Explanations recorded by a person, attributed, and retired when a later reconciliation moves the figure.
  • The gate on a close: a fund refuses its own NAV while a difference that matters is unexplained, a trade does not resolve, or a position is unpriced.
  • Books as the unit the console addresses — Personal, Fund, and Project templates — each job at its own URL. A Project book now cites budget vs actual and WIP capitalization over its own chart; Personal household figures and Investment partner capital are still the operations surface.

Designed, specified, not yet built

  • Both planes described on the platform page — the versioned control plane and the typed fact plane.
  • Postgres for transactional state. The journal is the store today; a relational engine is still ahead.
  • Every one of the sixteen capability areas below, at varying depths of specification.
  • The workload planner, the authoring assistant, and every interface shown anywhere on this site apart from one: an operations console exists, it addresses books, and it is still narrow — the exception queue, a difference at its own address, and whether a fund can close.

Two things are true at once, and it is worth stating both. The part that is hard to get right — a ledger that cannot go out of balance, and a way to prove it — is done. The part that is merely a lot of work is still mostly ahead. Anyone evaluating Ratio today is evaluating a foundation and a plan, not a product they could run a fund on this quarter.

The route to parity

Four phases, each gated on the one before

Ordered by dependency, not by demand. Nothing in a later phase can be built honestly before the one above it exists, which is why the sequence is what it is.

Phase zero — the core

Complete

The arithmetic, and the proof that it holds. The only phase whose correctness cannot be established by testing, and therefore the only one worth doing first.

  • Conservation kernel, proved in Lean 4
  • Proof-to-engine generation, drift-gated
  • Exact integer money with proved algebra
  • Service contract and balancing server

Parity reached: none yet — and by design. No incumbent advertises this, so there is nothing to be at parity with; it is the thing none of them can add later.

Phase one — books you can actually keep

Under way

The journal, the lots, the currencies, and the actions a first book needs are in the engine. What is still ahead is the two planes that decide and apply policy, and a first custodian feed that is not a file already in hand.

  • The append-only journal — persistence without a database
  • Control plane: versioned, reviewed, atomic
  • Fact plane: prices and FX with provenance
  • Multi-currency and FX translation
  • Tax lots and cost-basis methods
  • Corporate actions
  • Chart of accounts and posting rules
  • First custodian feed, end to end

Parity reached: the accounting half of portfolio accounting, and the ingest half of data aggregation and reconciliation — the two capabilities every incumbent leads with.

Phase two — the close

After

The month a fund accountant actually lives in. This is the phase where Ratio becomes usable rather than merely correct, and where a shadow run starts producing something worth comparing. Three lines below are already delivered, out of order and deliberately — the exception queue and the gate on a close were what a demo needed before it was worth looking at. Neither brings the rest of the phase any closer than it is.

  • Exception handling: differences measured against a declared threshold, explained by a person, retired when the figure moves
  • Reconciliation against live custodian feeds, at a fund's volume
  • The gate on a close: a fund refuses its own NAV while something is unexplained, unidentified or unpriced
  • NAV calculation and period close
  • Fee accrual and billing rules
  • Restatement and point-in-time reporting
  • Audit export and evidence trail
  • Workload planning and parallel execution
  • Rule authoring with grounded checks
  • Operations console: books (Personal, Fund, Project templates — a Project book cites budget vs actual and WIP capitalization), the exception queue, a difference at its own address, a book's state
  • The rest of the operations interface

Parity reached: billing and revenue management, the record-keeping half of compliance and surveillance, and the reconciliation half of data aggregation — plus reproducible history, which no incumbent offers at all.

Phase three — the desk

After

What advisors, portfolio managers and clients see. All of it reads the same contract any third party can read, so none of it needs privileged access to the ledger.

  • Performance measurement and attribution
  • Composites and standards-oriented reporting
  • Client and advisor portals
  • Models, drift and rebalancing
  • Tax-aware lot selection
  • Proposal generation and simulation
  • Practice analytics
  • Alternative-investment servicing

Parity reached: performance reporting and client portal, trading and rebalancing, models and managed accounts, proposal generation, practice analytics, alternative investments — the bulk of the advertised feature surface.

Phase four — the ecosystem

After

Breadth: the long tail of feeds, connectors and adjacent systems that took the incumbents twenty years, and the reason this phase is last rather than hard.

  • Custodian and administrator feed catalog
  • Market-data vendor coverage
  • CRM and practice-system connectors
  • Risk analytics and scenario modeling
  • Trust, retirement and insurance servicing
  • Access control and data governance
  • Third-party extension marketplace
  • Migration tooling from incumbent platforms

Parity reached: integrations and API, CRM, risk analytics, specialized servicing, data governance — closing the last of the sixteen.

On dates

There are no dates on this page, deliberately

A schedule is a function of how many people are working on something, and that is not currently a number worth publishing. Putting quarters against this list would communicate confidence that nobody has earned yet — and on a site whose entire argument is that you should check claims rather than accept them, an unbacked date would be the worst thing on it.

The ordering, though, is real. Each phase genuinely depends on the one above: you cannot reconcile before you can persist, you cannot report performance before you can close a period, and you cannot sell breadth before any of it. If a phase moves, the ones below it move with it.

If you need a date for a decision you are actually making, that is a conversation rather than a web page — and the honest answer will depend on what you need first.
