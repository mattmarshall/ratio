What you are living with

The work you do to make the system's numbers trustworthy

The platforms in this category are capable and entrenched, and most of them do the job. But a large share of what a good accounting team spends its month on is not accounting — it is compensating for a system nobody outside the vendor can look inside.

Breaks you resolve by hand

The custodian says one thing, the platform says another, and the spreadsheet says a third. There is no way to ask the system to show its work, so somebody reconciles it manually — and does it again next month.

A past that will not sit still

A price arrives late. A fee schedule changes. Suddenly last quarter's reported figure is not what the system produces today, and explaining the difference is an investigation rather than a lookup.

Differences you absorb rather than explain

Fractions of a cent accumulate across thousands of allocations, so the month ends with a small plug and a tolerance nobody loves. It is defensible. It is not explainable.

A bill indexed to your success

Pricing is usually a percentage of assets under management, so a good year raises your technology cost even though your usage has not moved. Then there is the headcount that exists to do the reconciling above.

An assistant sitting next to your numbers

Every vendor is adding AI. Very few can tell you what stops a confident, wrong suggestion from becoming an entry.

The issue is not the feature list — it is the foundation. You can buy more reports, more dashboards and more integrations. What you cannot buy from any incumbent is a set of books you are able to check yourself.

What changes

Five things that stop being your problem

Ratio is not a reporting layer on top of the same kind of engine. It replaces the engine, and these follow from that.

The books cannot go out of balance

Not “rarely do”. An entry that does not balance is refused at the door rather than written and caught later. There is no state in which the ledger is out and someone has to find it.

Any prior period reproduces exactly

Ask for 30 September as it stood on 30 September and you get it — under the fee schedule, prices and policies that were in force then, not today's. Restatements become a re-run instead of a reconstruction.

No rounding differences to absorb

Amounts are held exactly, in whole cents, and never as decimals a computer has to approximate. Totals do not drift with the order things were added in. There is no tolerance to set and no plug to book.

The audit request is a query

“Show me how this figure was calculated, under the policy in force that quarter.” That is a report Ratio can produce, not a project you staff. The record cannot be quietly edited after the fact.

The bill stops tracking the book

Priced on what you use, not on what you manage. It can run in your own environment, and your history exports in open, documented formats — so leaving is always possible, which is what makes staying a choice.

Re-run the performance pack. Answer the auditor's question about last March by finding whoever remembers it. Sign off, later than you wanted to.

The same close on Ratio

Load the same file. Anything that does not balance is rejected on the way in, with the reason attached, so the exception list is a list of real data problems rather than a mix of those and system artifacts. Nothing to plug. Last March re-runs on demand, exactly as reported, so the auditor's question is answered from the system rather than from memory.

What it costs

A bill that stops tracking your book

Most platforms in this category price as a percentage of assets under management. A strong year therefore raises your technology bill without changing what the technology does for you. On top of that sit implementation fees, per-seat add-ons, and the people whose job is to reconcile a system nobody can check.

Ratio prices on usage, and can run on your own infrastructure. The cost curve flattens as the book grows, and the reconciliation work shrinks instead of scaling with you.

About the AI

It can draft. It cannot touch a number.

Every vendor in this category is adding an assistant. The question worth asking is not whether they have one — it is what would happen if it were confidently wrong.

What it does for you

Turns a plain-language request into a precise instruction you can read before it runs. Drafts the client-facing explanation of a position, a fee or a performance figure. Suggests how an unusual transaction should be posted, and how a break might be resolved — as a proposal, for you to approve.

What it cannot do

Post an entry. Everything the assistant produces is checked against the same rules as any other entry before it can affect the books, and anything that does not balance is refused. The worst outcome from a bad suggestion is a suggestion you decline.

Where Ratio is today

What exists now, and what does not

Ratio is early. A page that asks you to check rather than trust would be a poor advertisement for itself if it were vague about its own state, so here it is plainly.

Working today

  • The accounting engine itself — entries that do not balance are refused, and the balancing guarantee has been independently checked rather than merely tested.
  • Exact amounts throughout, held in whole cents, with totals that do not depend on the order things were entered.
  • A documented interface other systems can read from and write to.

Not built yet

  • Custodian feeds, performance reporting, the client portal and the day-to-day screens your team would work in.
  • Multi-currency, tax lots and corporate actions — designed and specified, not yet implemented.
  • Fee billing, compliance and the rest of the surface the incumbents have spent twenty years accumulating.

In practice that means Ratio is not something you could run your firm on this quarter, and we would rather you heard that here than three meetings in. What it is ready for is a conversation about the foundation — and, when there is a feed to point at it, a shadow run.
