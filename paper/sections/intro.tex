\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:intro}

Double-entry bookkeeping has been the discipline's backbone since
Pacioli first printed it in 1494~\cite{pacioli1494summa}, and its
deep structure is group-theoretic: Ellerman showed that the
``T-accounts'' of double-entry form a group~\cite{ellerman1985mathematics}.
Accounting systems are foundational infrastructure: ledgers, sub-ledgers,
position keepers, and NAV engines underpin every financial institution.
They are held to an unusual standard --- \emph{absolute} arithmetic
correctness --- while simultaneously being asked to scale, to replay
history exactly for audit, and to absorb a continuous stream of new
business rules. In most production systems these demands are met by a
single, large, expressive codebase in which three concerns are fused:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item the \textbf{mechanical invariants} of accounting --- balance,
        conservation, deterministic aggregation;
  \item the \textbf{business semantics} --- posting rules, account
        mappings, normal-balance conventions, interpretation policy; and
  \item the \textbf{I/O} --- prices, FX rates, reference data, and the
        timing and provenance of each.
\end{enumerate}

When these share one mutable codebase, correctness becomes a
whole-program property. A change to a posting rule can violate an
arithmetic invariant; a late-arriving price can silently change a
historical balance; a refactor for performance can perturb semantics.
The invariant that \emph{should} be a three-line theorem is instead an
emergent, untestable property of hundreds of thousands of lines.

\paragraph{Thesis.} Ratio's claim is that the mechanical core of
accounting is not domain logic at all --- it is a \emph{conservation
law}, and conservation laws are exactly the kind of thing one proves
once and relies on forever. Concretely:

\begin{quote}
\itshape Economic computation is conservation over integer-valued vector
spaces. A transaction is an integer vector over a basis of conserved
dimensions; it is valid iff it nets to zero on every dimension; and the
ledger is a deterministic monoidal fold of the append-only log.
Conservation is closed under that fold, so the ledger conserves value
regardless of business semantics.
\end{quote}

This reframing buys three things that the entangled design cannot.
\textbf{Assurance:} the core is small enough to state as theorems and
\emph{machine-check} them --- we do, in Lean~4 (\cref{sec:proofs}).
\textbf{Determinism:} integers only, a fixed fold, immutable inputs ---
any ledger state replays bit-for-bit. \textbf{Performance:} a
non-expressive kernel over fixed-width records is branch-minimal and
memory-bandwidth bound, not control-flow bound (\cref{sec:kernel}).

\paragraph{What makes Ratio different from ``we wrote tests''.} The
kernel's correctness is not asserted by a test suite that samples the
input space; it is \emph{proved} for all inputs in a dependently-typed
proof assistant, and the Rust the application actually executes is
\emph{emitted from that same Lean source} rather than hand-translated
(\cref{sec:emission}). The proof and the artifact are not two documents
that drift; they are one. \Cref{fig:layers} shows the resulting
stratification: a proven kernel at the bottom, a content-addressed
control plane and typed-fact plane above it, and learned components
firmly outside the trusted core.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item A formalization of double-entry accounting as conservation over
        $\mathbb{Z}$-modules, generalizing ``debits $=$ credits'' to an
        arbitrary basis of conserved dimensions (\cref{sec:conservation}).
  \item A machine-checked proof, in Lean~4, that the ledger fold
        preserves conservation --- the kernel's sole invariant ---
        together with the recovery of classical double-entry as the
        one-dimensional instance (\cref{sec:proofs}).
  \item A \emph{proof-to-artifact} pipeline that emits the running Rust
        from the proven Lean, so the deployed kernel inherits the proof
        (\cref{sec:emission}).
  \item An architecture --- non-expressive kernel, content-addressed
        control plane, typed facts, LLM-as-assistant --- that delivers
        auditability and deterministic replay without sacrificing
        throughput (\cref{sec:control,sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}
