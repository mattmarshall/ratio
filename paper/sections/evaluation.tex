\section{Analysis and Value Proposition}
\label{sec:eval}

This section makes the value proposition quantitative. The figures here
are \emph{analytical models} of the architecture's asymptotics, not
measurements of a tuned deployment; they are drawn from the cost model of
\cref{sec:kernel} and are labeled as such. Their purpose is to show the
\emph{shape} of the trade Ratio makes, which is structural and therefore
predictable in advance of any benchmark.

\subsection{Throughput: bandwidth-bound, not logic-bound}

\Cref{fig:throughput} models per-transaction cost as the rule set grows.
An expressive engine dispatches on business cases on the hot path, so its
cost rises with rule-set complexity; the conservation kernel does a
fixed amount of integer work per fixed-width record, so its cost is flat.
The crossover is not a tuning artifact --- it is the difference between a
loop whose body depends on semantics and one whose body does not.

\subsection{Determinism: zero replay variance}

\Cref{fig:determinism} models replay dispersion. Because the kernel is
integer-only over immutable inputs with a fixed fold, repeated replays of
a log land on a single point --- variance is identically zero. An
order-sensitive or floating-point engine accumulates a small but nonzero
spread that compounds with log length, which is exactly what makes
bit-exact audit impossible in such systems. Determinism here is a
theorem of the construction, not a property one hopes survives
optimization.

\subsection{Assurance: a tiny trusted base}

\Cref{tab:trust} (\cref{sec:proofs}) and \cref{fig:layers}
(\cref{sec:intro}) tell the assurance story structurally: the trusted
computing base is the kernel's definitions plus the theorems over them,
and everything expressive --- control plane, fact plane, LLM-driven
authoring --- sits outside it, unable to violate conservation by
construction. The relevant comparison is not ``how many bugs were found''
but ``how large is the surface on which a correctness bug could exist at
all.'' Ratio shrinks that surface to a kernel small enough to prove, and
then proves it.

\subsection{Summary of the trade}

Ratio gives up engine-level expressiveness --- you cannot encode a
posting rule ``in the kernel'' --- and in exchange gets a core that is
(i) \emph{proved} correct for all inputs (\cref{thm:conservation}),
(ii) \emph{deterministically replayable} (\cref{fig:determinism}),
(iii) \emph{bandwidth-bound and fast} (\cref{fig:throughput}), and
(iv) faithfully \emph{realized} by emission rather than re-implementation
(\cref{sec:emission}). The expressiveness is not lost; it is relocated to
a content-addressed control plane where it is versioned, auditable, and
fenced away from the arithmetic it must never corrupt.
