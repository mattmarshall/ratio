\title{Ratio: A Formally Grounded Accounting Kernel\\
       \large {\normalfont Conservation laws, a non-expressive kernel,
       and machine-checked correctness for financial systems}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Production accounting systems entangle three things that should never
share a blast radius: the mechanical invariants of double-entry, the
business semantics that interpret them, and the I/O that feeds them.
The result is software where correctness cannot be reasoned about in
isolation and performance is unpredictable. \emph{Ratio} proposes a
different decomposition. We model economic computation as
\textbf{conservation over integer-valued vector spaces}: a transaction
is an integer vector over a basis of conserved dimensions, it is valid
exactly when it nets to zero on every dimension, and the ledger is a
deterministic monoidal fold of the append-only transaction log.
Conservation is preserved by aggregation, so the ledger is internally
consistent and exactly replayable \emph{independent of any business
logic}. We make this precise and \emph{prove} it: the core algebra and
the conservation law are mechanically checked in Lean~4, and the Rust
the application runs is \emph{emitted} from the same Lean source, closing
the gap between the proof and the artifact. All business semantics are
externalized to a versioned, content-addressed control plane; external
data enters as typed facts with explicit provenance; and large language
models are confined to translating intent into specifications they can
never override. The payoff is high assurance and high throughput at
once: a branch-minimal, fixed-width, integer-only kernel whose cost is
dominated by memory bandwidth rather than control-flow complexity.
\end{abstract}
