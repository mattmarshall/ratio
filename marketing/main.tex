\section*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}
{\color{ratioRule}\titlerule[1.2pt]}
\vspace{6pt}

Wealth-management technology runs on a paradox: the systems entrusted with the
most exacting arithmetic in finance are the ones their users can least inspect.
Performance reports, tax lots, multi-currency NAVs, and fee calculations emerge
from proprietary black boxes, reconciled by armies of operations staff and taken
on faith. \textbf{Ratio replaces faith with proof.}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Provably correct, not just well-tested.} Ratio's accounting
  kernel is modeled as a conservation law and \emph{machine-checked in Lean~4};
  the running engine is generated from those proofs, so the math your clients
  depend on is verified for all inputs, not sampled by a test suite.
  \item \textbf{Auditable by construction.} An append-only, integer-exact ledger
  with a content-addressed control plane means any historical state replays
  bit-for-bit, under the exact rules in force at the time --- audit and
  point-in-time reporting become a query, not a forensic project.
  \item \textbf{AI-native, safely.} LLMs translate intent into specifications and
  draft explanations, but are architecturally fenced out of the trusted core:
  a model can propose, never corrupt the ledger.
  \item \textbf{Auditable, extensible, and unbundled from AUM.} A readable Rust
  core your reviewers can check, open data formats you can export in full, and
  pricing independent of assets under management --- the opposite of legacy
  lock-in.
\end{itemize}
