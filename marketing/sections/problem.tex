\section{The Incumbent Stack Runs on Trust, Not Proof}

For two decades, advisors, family offices, and asset managers have standardized
on a handful of portfolio-accounting and performance-reporting platforms ---
SS\&C Black Diamond, Orion, Addepar, Envestnet Tamarac, and their peers. These
systems are capable and entrenched. They are also, structurally, \emph{black
boxes}: closed engines whose correctness you cannot inspect, whose history you
cannot reproduce independently, and whose roadmap and pricing you do not control.

That arrangement was acceptable when there was no alternative. It is increasingly
expensive in four concrete ways:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Correctness is asserted, not demonstrated.} The arithmetic behind
  a performance figure or a tax lot is proprietary. When numbers disagree across
  systems --- custodian vs.\ platform vs.\ spreadsheet --- the resolution is
  manual reconciliation, not a proof. Operations teams exist largely to paper
  over a trust gap.
  \item \textbf{History is not reproducible.} Restating a prior period, answering
  ``what did we report on this date and under which rules,'' or surviving an audit
  means reconstructing state from logs and memory. Late-arriving prices and silent
  rule changes can alter the past without a trace.
  \item \textbf{You are locked in --- and billed on your success.} Data lives in
  the vendor's format; integrations route through the vendor's gates; and pricing
  is typically a function of assets under management, so your platform bill rises
  with your book even when your usage does not.
  \item \textbf{AI is bolted on, not built in safely.} The incumbents are racing
  to add LLM features atop engines never designed to contain them --- exactly the
  architecture in which a confident, wrong model can quietly move a number.
\end{itemize}
